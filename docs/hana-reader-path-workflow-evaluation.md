# Reader-path checks in article verification

Evaluation date: October 18, 2026. Baseline website revision:
`02d3897f9f5bc4c80c9cb97b2f30a19a54731be1`.

Hana's critiques in issues #98, #99, #100 and #103 repeatedly ask whether a reader
knows the destination, can distinguish neighboring terms, and can follow a change
of example or interpret dense evidence. These are useful additions to the existing
teaching review. They do not justify a standard introduction, glossary or outline
for every article.

The workflow brief now records assumed knowledge and the reader's path. The
review prompt asks for concrete difficulties in orientation, terminology,
transitions, evidence framing, methodological examples and Markdown diagram
explanations. Revision verification checks whether the repair helps without
introducing repetition, ceremony or a weaker authorial position. Blog-writing
remains unchanged. Dates retain their existing preservation contract.

## Limited trial

Four non-archival article revisions and one unchanged reference control supplied
different teaching forms. The initial complete baseline reviews preceded the
outside critiques. Those critiques were then adjudicated against the texts and
sources. Fresh comparisons read complete baseline/candidate pairs before the
dispositions, using the revised reader-path verification prompt. The unchanged
control received the revised teaching-review prompt in a fresh session.

The comparison of Factory and Skills was restarted with a clean packet after a
reviewer disclosed reading handoff material. The first Factory review was also
excluded after accidental exposure to repository-wide snippets. Neither exposed
report supplies the accepted independent verdicts. The clean reviewers received
the relevant project constraints through the coordinator's packet.

The trials also rejected unsupported readings of the critiques. Skills' review
example adds coverage and evidence-separation lessons; it does not nearly double
the article. Its text graph already explains the expense procedure in Markdown.
Factory's first-person naming convention already states its reason. The eval
article already announces the published-skill obligation near the opening; a
glossary and a second announcement were unnecessary. These rejections matter:
reader-path checks should improve the actual article rather than enforce a form.

This was a selective editorial trial, with the outside critiques available during
adjudication. It was not a blinded seeded-defect benchmark. It supplies no measured
detection rate or claim that future teaching problems will all be found. No known
seeded-issue miss was measured. The initial independent reviews found the worked
eval prompt needed naming and the Stampede capacity/evidence distinctions needed
clarification; the outside critiques supplied additional supported reading aids.
The control produced no required edit. Final comparisons reported no regressions.

No original blog source, historical publication copy, PWI chapter, tested code,
recorded output or artwork changed in this workflow trial. The workflow changes
are committed separately from the subsequent authorized article revisions.
