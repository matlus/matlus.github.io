# Article story arc

Use this checklist while drafting every article, during independent teaching
review, and when verifying a revision. It extends the reader-path checks in the
[publication workflow](article-publication-workflow.md). The nine checks come
from [issue #131](https://github.com/matlus/matlus.github.io/issues/131), including
Shiv's addition about limits. They guide editorial judgment; they make no claim
about a reader's psychology or a measured increase in engagement.

Read the complete article first and identify its reader, form and promised
outcome. A reference, short practical note, walkthrough and sustained argument
need different amounts of narrative. Check all nine items, recording **met**,
**needs revision**, or **not applicable**, with passages or a reason. A justified
not-applicable decision is valid. Do not add a personal anecdote, antagonist,
metaphor or conclusion merely to fill a slot. Historical originals and PWI
chapters retain their protection under the publication workflow.

## The nine checks

1. **Establishing shot with shared confusion.** Put the reader inside the
   situation where the problem occurs. Establish the setting, people or system,
   stakes and detail to watch quickly. Make the difficulty recognizable without
   treating the learner as foolish. Include the author's learning experience
   when it is supplied and useful. A hypothetical example can establish shared
   confusion without claiming it happened to Shiv.
2. **Hook.** Read the first line and opening together: can a new reader identify
   the subject and a reason to continue? Expose a consequential surprise, failed
   expectation or question that the article answers. A short reference can earn
   attention through its immediate practical purpose. Do not manufacture suspense
   or withhold prerequisites to make an opening intriguing.
3. **Illustrative word pictures.** Can the reader picture what happens at the
   demanding points in the explanation? Prefer concrete objects, actions, inputs
   and observable results to summary words. An analogy should make a relationship
   easier to understand. The read-aloud, eyes-closed test can expose abstraction;
   colour, pattern, texture and shine are useful when they teach something.
   Exact code, data or a worked trace can supply the picture. Decorative sensory
   details and new image files are not requirements.
4. **Structure matched to the intended feeling.** Identify the progression and
   why it suits the lesson: satisfaction and rhythm, urgency, transformation or
   causal clarity. Possible tools include Save the Cat, problem/agitation/solution,
   a hero's journey and and/but/therefore. Use them as planning aids when helpful;
   preserve foundation before solution and an order in which each step can be
   understood. No named structure or dramatic conflict is mandatory.
5. **Promises paid off (Chekhov's gun).** Track emphasized details, questions,
   examples and promised results through to their payoff. Name each passage's
   job: orient, explain, provide proof, answer an objection or establish a
   consequence. Remove purposeless detail and resolve abandoned threads. Keep
   context and deliberate returns that do useful teaching work; this is not an
   instruction to compress every sentence or create artificial urgency.
6. **Villain named early.** Identify the concrete failure, misconception or
   constraint the reader needs to overcome. Where a sustained argument benefits,
   develop its consequences, external causes, attempted remedies and why those
   remedies failed. Use only supported causes and actual or clearly hypothetical
   attempts. The obstacle can be a confident but unchecked answer or an ambiguous
   contract; it need not be a person. Do not invent blame, failed solutions or
   hostility to make a technical lesson dramatic.
7. **Medicine and sugar.** Identify the necessary learning and the experience
   that helps the reader stay with it: solving a recognizable problem, following
   a concrete example, discovering a cause or hearing the author's direct voice.
   Give dense data and process an understandable role in that progression. The
   explanation still needs its full reasoning and evidence; personality cannot
   replace a missing step or soften a firm, supported rule.
8. **Bookend.** When the article develops a sustained lesson, return to an opening
   image, question or belief with the understanding the reader has now gained.
   The return should answer or change something, rather than repeat a slogan.
   Remove the return mentally: if no resolution is lost, strengthen its purpose
   or omit it. A lookup reference or tiny practical note can finish at its useful
   result without a separate closing scene.
9. **Limits beat.** State what the example or result establishes and what it
   leaves unmeasured, where those limits affect the reader's interpretation.
   Keep that honesty through the explanation. A passing check on one model,
   configuration, input or environment does not establish another. Distinguish
   observed results, proposed experiments and personal experience. Add neither
   unsupported certainty nor generic caveats to make the ending sound balanced.

## How the checklist enters publication

The brief identifies the obstacle, reader outcome, intended progression and
opening/ending connection where applicable. The writer checks the complete draft.
The fresh teaching reviewer then assesses all nine independently, without the
writer's expected verdicts. The writer adjudicates findings against passages and
evidence. The fresh comparison reviewer checks the candidate for improvement,
new unsupported claims and narrative additions that obstruct the lesson.

Before calling an article ready, retain the final nine-item assessment and the
disposition of every needs-revision item in the editorial receipt. Correct
consequential unresolved findings before publishing, or obtain the owner's
decision when the remaining disagreement affects meaning. A clean copy audit
does not establish that the story arc works. Rendered inspection and the other
publication gates still apply.
