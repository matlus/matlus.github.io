# Article publication workflow: initial evaluation

Evaluation date: October 13, 2026. Shiv requested implementation and trials on
four or five varied new articles, preserving the original blog articles and
committing workflow changes separately from later article revisions.

## Method and scope

The site baseline was `b02303498e8cc06fcd926f520d7cd0c30617ad78`.
Five complete article snapshots were placed in an external trial directory.
Four were current copies; Expense Analysis, Part Three used the pre-edit
`7a865be714af7dd19b1cad6df8df531e165323fe` snapshot for recovery testing.
The current Part Three was reviewed separately as a repaired-version control.
Neither original blog source material nor its published copies was revised.

Reviews used fresh sub-agent contexts with no drafting conversation, prior
critiques or expected findings. Inputs included a reader/purpose brief, full
article, relevant adjacent parts and available primary source evidence. All
73 source headings across the five cases were documented in coverage records;
each review also recorded the article ending. Coverage records demonstrate
access and do not prove comprehension.

The coordinator checked findings against passages and sources, recorded accepted,
rejected and deferred items, and passed only supported changes to the revision
stage. Candidates stayed outside the public content collection. Separate fresh
contexts compared baseline and candidate before reading previous conclusions.
There were two revision/comparison cycles for the expense case and one for the
others. The configured model was used in separate contexts; this was not a
cross-vendor benchmark or a measured general detection rate.

### Findings that changed the workflow

- The initial pipeline review created an unnecessary corroboration task for
  supplied personal experience. That task was rejected. The review prompt now
  treats the owner's account as its source unless evidence conflicts or the
  prose makes a broader measurable claim. A new cold pipeline review proposed
  no substantive findings.
- The first expense review caught the $24.10 arithmetic gap but missed the
  ambiguous original-key/diagnostic-key chronology. A second cold review after
  explicitly strengthening comparison/version tracking identified that issue.
  Reviewer agreement or one pass cannot establish complete coverage.
- The first expense candidate fixed the arithmetic but gave unsupported
  specificity to the retained claim about four successful runs. The independent
  comparison held it as unresolved: all four saved answers reported $1,631.82.
  The second revision corrected the observation and passed a new comparison.
- A metadata session encountered earlier conclusions in the project handoff.
  Its extraction was stopped and was not counted as independent. A replacement
  session used an isolated packet containing articles, siblings, vocabulary and
  the extraction prompt. The workflow now specifies clean packets and disclosure
  of context exposure.
- The clean metadata trial produced five outputs and no new tags. Its Jev
  description used wording that could imply a recommended local evaluation had
  already happened. That wording was rejected; metadata acceptance now explicitly
  checks the distinction between recommendations and performed measurements.
  Trial frontmatter was left unchanged.

## Adoption and later article work

The trials support adopting the separate editorial workflow with human/coordinator
adjudication and independent revision comparison. They also demonstrate missed
findings and imperfect proposed changes, so the workflow is not certification
that every article defect will be detected. Unchanged articles are a legitimate
outcome.

Only workflow instructions, prompts, routing documentation and this evaluation
record are included in the workflow commit. No public article, description, tag,
image, source archive, blog-writing skill or deployment job is changed. GitHub
deployment continues to run automated checks without starting editorial sessions.

After the workflow is merged, later article tasks can consider the retained
findings. Expense work includes the reported successful-run total, source/grader
distinctions and comparison scope. SDLC work includes research provenance and the
intended AI/human authorship policy. Jev work includes normalization, experiment
scope and dated source records. Pipeline and delegates provenance tasks require
their original recordings rather than speculative prose edits. Article changes
remain separate from this workflow adoption.

## Date-preservation follow-up

Shiv requested that article edits preserve both publication and update dates,
including the absence of either field. The workflow and revision/comparison
prompts now state this explicitly. The previous general instruction to record
editorial work in dateModified was removed. Revision history remains in Git.

A fresh revision session applied the same small, supported Score clarification
to five isolated Jev copies: both dates present and equal, dates present but
different, no update date, no publication date, and neither date present.
All five candidates changed prose while preserving their complete frontmatter
exactly. Copies without the required publication field stayed outside the public
content collection; the schema was unchanged.

A separate fresh comparison checked those five pairs plus four deliberately
altered candidates. It identified all four date regressions: adding an absent
update date, adding an absent publication date, changing an existing update date,
and removing an existing update date. It found no regression in the five
date-preserving revisions. These are focused examples, not a general detection
guarantee.

Candidate writing audits found no new flags; two existing copy advisories were
retained as substantive distinctions in unchanged prose. All six repository
publication checks passed again. The homepage HTML was byte-identical before
and after the build, and the follow-up diff contains no public article, asset,
schema, sorting or deployment changes. The cold comparison correctly left
rendered date inspection pending because its packet contained no rendered pages.
The date-trial inputs, outputs and exact comparisons are retained under
`verification/date-preservation/` in the external workspace above.
