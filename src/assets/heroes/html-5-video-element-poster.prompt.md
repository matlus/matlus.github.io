# Html 5 Video Element Poster

Hand-drawn technical editorial illustration, fine pen-and-ink linework and cross-hatching, warm ivory paper background, mostly monochrome with sparse selective color accents, visible pencil construction marks, slightly retro instructional-manual character, sophisticated conceptual metaphor, clean white space, detailed but not photorealistic.

Article idea: The HTML5 video poster should remain until playback begins. A 2010 critique explains why selected thumbnails matter more than automatically showing the first frame.
Subject: An editor's desk with a row of film thumbnails under a magnifying loupe. A hand selects one expressive scene card and places it in the front of a browser video-player frame; behind it sits a dark first-frame card that would reveal little about the film. A small closed play control makes clear that playback has not begun. The chosen representative poster is central, blue frame with amber selected thumbnail, no readable labels.
Composition: one wide landscape scene; all important objects and their connection occupy the middle horizontal third so a very wide banner crop retains the relationship. Airy margins above and below. No text, code, labels, logos, or watermark.
