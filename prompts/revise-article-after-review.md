# Revise an article after review

Use the blog-writing skill and the article publication workflow. Inputs are the
complete draft, reader and author brief, source evidence, the independent review,
and an explicit list of protected material. Revise only the authorized article.

First verify each substantive finding against the draft and evidence. Classify
it as accepted, rejected, deferred for evidence, or optional. Explain the decision
with a passage or source reference. A reviewer verdict alone does not justify a
change. Keep useful repetition, deliberate questions and the author's conviction.

Apply the smallest effective correction for accepted findings. Do not convert
unverified claims into errors or add generic caveats to an established argument.
An acceptable draft can remain unchanged. Record optional suggestions separately
when their benefit is uncertain. For tests, write a candidate copy outside the
published content collection; keep the baseline untouched.

Preserve the article's title, public URL, original date, tested prompts, recorded
outputs, code, numbers and reference tables unless the authorized change and
source evidence specifically require changing them. Keep existing anchors when
renaming or moving headings. Preserve technical qualifications, personal
experience and conclusions. Do not edit the recovered original blog articles,
their publication copies, PWI chapters or source archives through this workflow.

Return the revised draft or unchanged decision, a finding-disposition record,
and a concise account of changed passages. Identify any source conflict that
cannot be resolved. Run the applicable writing and copy audits, review their
flags in context, and do not rewrite protected evidence just to pass an audit.
