# Verify an article revision

Use in a fresh, read-only session. Inputs are the baseline and candidate article,
reader and author brief, relevant source evidence, protected-material list, and
the review findings with their dispositions. Compare the complete texts before
reading the prior review's conclusions. Use the blog-writing skill for voice.

For each actual change, determine whether it improves understanding or accuracy
and whether it introduces a regression. Check the example's inputs, reasoning,
arithmetic, chronology and results. Check the argument, scope, personal experience,
purposeful questions and useful qualifications. A shorter sentence or smoother
transition is not sufficient evidence of improvement.

Verify that accepted findings are resolved. Identify harmful suggestions that
were correctly rejected and unresolved issues without assuming every suggestion
needed an edit. Check that no necessary explanation was lost when repetition
was removed or sections moved. Compare tested prompts, code, recorded outputs,
reference tables, title, original date, and established anchors. Explain any
authorized evidence-backed change to these protected elements.

Give a verdict: improved, equivalent, regressed, or unresolved. Cite changed
passages and evidence supporting the verdict. Distinguish inspected facts from
unverified source claims. Do not infer success from an empty automated audit.
Report regressions ahead of preferences. Do not edit either version.

If no changes were warranted, an equivalent verdict is a successful outcome.
If a regression is found, return it to the revision step. Inspect the final
rendered article separately after its last prose or layout change; an earlier
page inspection does not cover a later candidate.
