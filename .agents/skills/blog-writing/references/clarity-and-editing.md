# Shared clarity and editing principles

These principles carry forward the professional-writing foundation. Blog-writing adds Shiv's voice and teaching flow; professional-writing remains an independent, general-purpose skill.

Preserve facts, technical distinctions, qualifications, commitments, and the author's position. Never invent evidence, experience, opinions, or certainty. Remove empty hedging while keeping uncertainty that matters. Preserve the force of a supplied rule and place qualifications where they help the reader understand or apply it. Do not add hypothetical caveats as a reflex. Prefer supported specifics to vague praise.

Give the reader the article's purpose early. Develop each point with the explanation it needs. Remove unnecessary introductions and accidental repetition. Keep a deliberate return to an earlier example when it now explains something new. Keep summaries when the requested format or reader's decision benefits from them.

Use plain, literal language. Avoid decorative abstractions, canned praise, slogans, invented labels, mechanical transitions, and stock closing offers. Keep an analogy when it helps explain something unfamiliar, and a contrast when it distinguishes real alternatives. Avoid setting up an invented opposing claim merely to reject it.

Questions must earn their place in the explanation. A question about why code behaves a certain way, followed by an example that explains it, is useful teaching. Repeated rhetorical questions used only to announce headings make the writing mannered. Preserve the former and revise the latter.

Use concrete nouns and active verbs when they clarify responsibility. Vary sentence lengths and openings naturally. Do not enforce pronoun patterns, sentence-length formulas, or an arbitrary conversational rhythm. Use connected paragraphs for reasoning, lists for parallel items or steps, and headings or tables when they make the material easier to follow. Keep bold restrained. Add emoji or hashtags only when requested.

For newly written prose, use ordinary punctuation; do not introduce em dashes or single-character ellipses. Use semicolons sparingly and three periods only when a trailing-off effect is intended. Preserve required quotations, identifiers, and original source text. Current explicit wording choices from Shiv take precedence over these defaults.

On the final pass, check meaning, repetition, unsupported claims, and phrasing that draws attention to itself. Improve clarity without flattening an opinion, deleting a useful qualification, or compressing away a necessary explanation. Return the deliverable with only the editorial context the user needs.
